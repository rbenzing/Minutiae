# Limitations: parsers and the parse host

What the current build does not do, does approximately, or has validated only on builder data, for the parse contract and host (`internal/parse`, `internal/artparse`), `internal/recordtypes`, the decoders `decode/ts`, `decode/plist`, `decode/typedstream`, `decode/iosbackup` and `decode/tl`, and the artifact parsers in `internal/parsers`. Part of the [known limitations](../limitations.md), which say how to add or close one.

- `case verify` and parse jobs (plan 4H, `artparse.ParseJobsCheck`): the command line cross-checks every ingest of a `parse` or `parse-recover` analysis against the `parse.job.start` before it (parser identity and bundle artifacts) and every `parse.job.end` against its ingest (PJ1-PJ5): a mismatch, a duplicate or a job end that disagrees with its ingest is exit 4, a concluded run with an unconcluded job is exit 4, a run that died before its job's ingest started or whose ingest `records.ingest.recover` concluded is a notice, and a `parse.job.end` without an ingest id over a job whose ingest started (a failed `Writer.Start` after `records.ingest.start`) is a notice when its outcome is not `complete` and the ingest was concluded by `records.ingest.recover` or is still unconcluded, and a problem when it says `complete` or the ingest was concluded by `records.ingest.end` or `records.ingest.error`; an ingest nothing concluded stays the lifecycle check's problem. Residuals: a library caller of `Case.VerifyWith` that does not compose `artparse.ParseJobsCheck` gets no notice (the command line always does); a run cut between a job's `records.ingest.end` and its `parse.job.end` is a notice (the ingest itself is concluded and verified by the lifecycle check); a run that stopped on an audit failure (`analysis.error` text `the run stopped: audit-failure`) with the LAST started job unended is a notice, so a forger who can rechain the log can hide exactly one missing job end this way (only the last job, and only with that error text; the anchor of `case anchor` covers it), while every other unended job of a concluded run, and a job started before the previous one ended, is a problem.
- Artifact parsers (sub-project 4, plan 4A: contract, host, registry, `parse list|plan|run`). The registry holds the three Android telephony parsers of plan 4D (`android.mmssms`, `android.calllog`, `android.contacts`), the iOS message, call history and address book parsers of plan 4E (`ios.sms`, `ios.calls`, `ios.addressbook`) the two browser history parsers of plan 4F (`chromium.history`, `safari.history`) and the two Android WhatsApp parsers of plan 4I (`android.whatsapp.msgstore`, then `android.whatsapp.contacts`) and the three iOS WhatsApp parsers of plan 4I2 (`ios.whatsapp.chats`, `ios.whatsapp.calls`, `ios.whatsapp.contacts`, appended last in that order) and the two calendar parsers of plan 4I3 (`android.calendar`, then `ios.calendar`) and the Telegram Android parser of plan 4I4 (`android.telegram`) and the Google Messages parser of plan 4I5 (`android.googlemessages`, appended last); the registry holds seventeen parsers; the three Android telephony parsers declare claims for the tables they map (`android.mmssms`: `sms`, `pdu`; `android.calllog`: `calls`; `android.contacts`: `raw_contacts`, `deleted_contacts`; plan 4G Tasks 9 and 10) the two 4F browser parsers declare theirs (`chromium.history`: `visits`, `keyword_search_terms`, `downloads`; `safari.history`: `history_visits`; Task 10b) and the two 4I parsers declare theirs (`android.whatsapp.msgstore`: `message`, `messages`, `call_log`; `android.whatsapp.contacts`: `wa_contacts`) while of the 4E parsers `ios.calls` declares `ZCALLRECORD` and `call` (plan 4H Task 2, `ioscalls.TestClaimsEqualClaimableTables`) and `ios.sms` declares `message` (4H; proven by `TestClaimedTablesMapRecoveredRows` over `ios17-deleted` and `ios17-wal`) and `ios.addressbook` declares `ABPerson` (plan 4H Task 3, proven by `iosaddressbook.TestClaimedTablesMapRecoveredRows`), so the tests that range over claims (`TestClaimsEqualMappings`, `TestClaimedTablesEmptyWithoutClaims`) have the real claiming case as well as their self-test tables, and `TestRegistryWellFormed`, `TestRepositoryIdentitiesAreCurrent` and `TestRegisteredParsersClaimExactly` (an explicit table of every registered parser and its claims, 4G-R30; since plan 4H Task 5 it also fails a parser that claims nothing, so every first-set parser claims) range over the real registry, and `TestEveryFirstSetParserClaimsItsClaimableTables` pins that the fourteen parsers exporting `ClaimableTables()` claim exactly it, in order, on role `db`. `parse recover` (the typed recovery pass of the claiming parsers; `--min-confidence` narrows it and makes it incomplete) and `--include-recovered` (on `parse plan`, `run` and `recover`) exist (plan 4G Task 11): `parse plan` counts the recovered or carved artifacts it leaves out (`N recovered or carved artifacts are not discovered`) and marks each included one `[recovered:<method> conf N]` (`conf unknown` when the derivation chain is cut), while `parse run` does not repeat that count; `parse plan --scan` probes every selected parser on eligible artifacts that no glob matched (one hash per artifact, up to 1 GiB; companions and larger files counted, non-databases counted as not applicable, unsupported-schema, encrypted and probe failures listed as findings, at most 1000); candidates run only with `--artifact`; an abandoned Probe, a cancel or an I/O failure stops the scan (`stopped` abandoned, cancelled or failed, the rest counted `not_probed`; after an abandoned Probe or an I/O failure partway through a candidate's parsers the pairs of that candidate that never ran are counted `pairs_not_probed`, `TestScanIOErrorCountsTheCandidatesRemainingProbes`); `candidates` counts artifacts with a `candidate` row (an already-parsed or stale-bundle row is in the by-status counts); the cost is one read of every considered artifact for the primary (hashed once) plus one read of its companions per parser (the `ios-backup` namer and backup resolution exist since plan 4E, see "iOS backup mapping"); `parse plan` and `parse run` print one `BACKUP` line per iOS backup (JSON `backups`, always an array) with its status, encryption state and counts, and a Manifest ref there means identified, not verified; snapshot-derived artifacts are parsed only with `--include-snapshots`; a parser can emit only types that have a payload contract; the validators are active in every binary that links `artparse`. The iOS parsers (`ios.sms`, `ios.calls`, `ios.addressbook`) are validated only against fixtures built from schemas written from memory (see "iOS schemas and backup formats are from memory"); the `realdata` harness for running them over real device data exists and no run is recorded (see "Real-data runs"). `analysis.start.backups` is an array for `parse run` and `parse recover` (`backups_resolved` true for the latter); audit logs written before plan 4H hold `backups: null` with `backups_resolved` false for `parse recover` (unknown, never an empty array). The shared parser fuzz helpers (`parsertest.FuzzParse`, `FuzzParseIOS`, `FuzzParseBundle`) fail on a record the writer refused (4E, Task 16). The Android telephony and iOS parsers state a REAL in an integer column as `type-mismatch` without its bits (the stored bytes are at the record's range); a shared helper is a backlog item.
- Parser isolation is in-process. A stack overflow or an out-of-memory kills the process (`debug.SetMemoryLimit`, set to twice the memory budget for the run and restored, is a soft GC limit only). The threat model is hostile DATA, not a malicious parser author. A parser that ignores cancellation is ABANDONED: it is sealed, its accepted records are flushed, its ingest is concluded as error and the whole run stops (exit 1, "restart recommended"); the spinning goroutine keeps a core and the memory its inputs pin until the process exits. Flush and Abort wait at most the grace period behind a stuck writer call, then the job is abandoned with that reason. The run summary `Stopped` is `abandoned`, `cancelled`, `audit-failure`, `abort-failure` or `run-error` (any other run-level failure such as `ErrIngestActive` or a failed `Start`). Measured (plan 4H, `docs/performance.md`, Windows only): a 200 MiB database parses in memory with a peak resident set of 545 MiB (counting writer) and 698 MiB (records writer), and a 1.25 GiB database streams at a 256 MiB budget within 136 MiB; 500 bundles at that budget peak at 94 MiB. The peak is the whole test process, so it is an upper bound; the Linux and unmeasured paths were not run. Decided in plan 4H (spec 17 Q7): parsers stay in-process and compiled in; no worker process, and plan 4J is not opened. The evidence is the first decoder fuzz campaign (12 targets, no finding), the bounded-work hostile-input tests, the bounded audit and warning volume on hostile mutations (`TestHostileAuditVolumeIsBounded`) and the measured memory above. Revisit if: a fuzz or real-data finding that the in-process controls cannot contain (a stack overflow, an out-of-memory despite the budget, a goroutine that cannot be abandoned); the post-LX-8 parser fuzz campaigns (pending a Docker VM of at least 4 GiB) find such a case; or the first app parser needs a native decoder.
  The conclusion of an abandoned job runs detached from the caller's context and `Run` joins it (at most `ConcludeTimeout`) before it audits its end and returns. A conclusion still pending then (a stuck writer) makes `Run` return `ErrConclusionPending`; the job stays reported incomplete and the next Start recovers the unconcluded ingest.
- Parse outcomes: an integrity failure while opening a job's inputs makes the job `refused` with `Integrity` true (not `unparsed`), the run goes on and the final exit is 4, also next to a cancel (integrity wins); a plain I/O error opening inputs leaves the job `unparsed` and the exit at least 1. A job whose writer refused records (`CheckRefusals`) concludes `incomplete` with reason `records-refused`, so it cannot supersede an older complete run; the cost is a re-run to supersede. `warnings` and `rejected` are in `records.ingest.end` only (not in `records stats`), and an interrupted ingest's recovery derives its counts from the audit log (`suppression_unknown` when the cap was hit before the conclusion note was written). A re-hash of the inputs that times out (its own 5 minute budget, apart from the conclusion's) concludes the job `incomplete` with reason `recheck-timeout`: an error, exit 1, never an integrity verdict. A record of the run's snapshot that is missing from the manifest, or a manifest that cannot be read, when a job opens its inputs is an integrity failure (exit 4). A bundle that gained a companion artifact since an earlier run is `unparsed` as `stale-bundle` unless `--reparse` is given (`TestParseExitCodes`).
- Recovered artifacts as parser input (plan 4G): `recover` and `carve` artifacts, and any artifact whose derivation chain holds a recovery description (`evidence.RecoveryTrailOf`, for example an `extract` of a recovered file), are parser input only with `--include-recovered` (`Selection.IncludeRecovered`) or when named with `--artifact`; `slack`, `journal`, `report`, `runs`, `unallocated`, images, `info` and `serial` are never input. The parser sees the trail in `Artifact.Recovery`: the class and method of the nearest recovered artifact and the LOWEST confidence on the chain; when the chain is cut (a missing parent, a cycle, the depth bound) or no link carries a confidence the cap is unknown (`CapUnknown`), and every record is capped at 25 with the note `recovery-cap-unknown`. A recovered artifact without a method in its description gets the method `unknown`. The recovered namer gives a recovered file the path its filesystem gave it, with the extract namer's platform inference; a carved artifact has no filesystem path and so no logical path (only `--artifact` reaches it). Propagation is per job, not per artifact: when ANY member of a bundle is recovered, every record of the job is stamped recovered and capped by the lowest cap of any recovered member, because a live `-wal` read over a recovered database may describe a state the database never had; a record on a live member of such a bundle carries `payload.recovery.relation: "from-recovered-bundle"`, `bundle_member` (the recovered artifact that bounds the cap), `artifact_method`, and the note `mixed-bundle`, and the job notes `mixed_bundle: <recovered ids>`. A live parser can no longer set `Record.Recovery` or `Record.Confidence` (the emitter refuses and counts it, so the job is incomplete); the host alone stamps them. An artifact with a recovered ancestor is no longer discovered by default (it used to be taken as live): re-run `parse run --include-recovered` to read it, and its records are recovered records.
- Parse input trust window. Large inputs (over 256 MiB) are pre-hashed and re-hashed after the job, not continuously, and a streamed input is served only through a section of the verified size (it never grows past it). An input changed during the job and restored before the re-hash (ABA) is NOT detected for a streamed input (`job.end` marks it `streamed: true`); an input changed and not restored is caught by the re-hash (exit 4, the run is `incomplete` with `integrity: true`, batches committed during that job stay, and `case verify` flags the artifact). The exclusive case lock is the defence against another process. The manifest is read once per run, but decoded again per opened artifact (O(artifacts x manifest) until an open-by-record function exists; 5D backlog), and `parse plan` reads every candidate artifact once. Discovery trusts the manifest's `Source` fields (the CLI prints a note: run `case verify` first). The platform of a `file` artifact is known only from device evidence in the case (an `ios pull` with no `ios backup` or `ios info` evidence has none, so only `--artifact` names it); the iOS extract path mapping covers only `/private/var/mobile/`. Measured (plan 4H, `docs/performance.md`): a 1.25 GiB streamed input (5,460,000 rows) was pre-hashed, parsed in 96 s and re-hashed with the memory line held; the whole window between pre-hash and re-hash is that parse time, one run on one machine.
- Parser handles: a parser receives its own `BudgetView` handle onto the invocation's accounting (`Input.Clone`), so overwriting it (`*in.Budget = parse.BudgetView{}`) stops only that handle's further allocations (they fail closed with `ErrBudget`). That is all it protects: the host keeps no handle of its own and settles nothing at the end of a job, its limits do not depend on the view, and `parse.NewBudget` is exported, so the budget is cooperative accounting for the decoders a trusted parser hands it to, not a boundary against a parser author. `Input.Lookup` is an interface whose only implementation is unexported. The threat model is hostile DATA with trusted parser code (the purity rules forbid `reflect` and `unsafe`). A read in flight when the host seals a parser's reader returns `ErrSealed` with no byte count but may already have filled the parser's buffer; the host never reads that buffer.
- Parser metadata: `InputSpec.Companions` fills a NON-primary role from the primary's path plus the suffix; the primary declares none, a suffix names at most one role, and a role that can never be filled is refused by `ValidateMeta` (`TestValidateMetaRefusesCompanionsOnThePrimaryRole` and its siblings); the host does not check that the primary is a SQLite database, a title is at most 200 bytes of valid UTF-8 without control characters, a claiming parser's name is at most 56 bytes (so `<name>.recover` fits the 64-byte parser name), and no parser name ends in `.recover` (`TestValidateMetaRefusesLongClaimingName`, `TestValidateMetaRefusesRecoverSuffix`).
- Parser identity: the hash covers layout-, comment- and import-grouping-insensitive syntax, so any other change, including a formatter's `0777` to `0o777` and `a string, b string` to `a, b string` rewrites, needs a version bump; a Go release that adds AST fields can change a dump only for source that uses them. `internal/parse` and `internal/records` (the parsers use their constants and data types) are not in the hash scope: a change there is a change of the build, not of a parser version. A parser package that embeds a file keeps it raw. The recover identity of a claiming parser (`<name>.recover`) follows the same lock rule and shares the parser's version: a runner change forces a version bump of every claiming parser. `parsers.lock` is append-only and has no external anchor: someone with file access can edit it, the writer still refuses a conflicting identity per case. `Start` runs the full schema-object comparison once per job (measure with hundreds of jobs). The CLI prints at most one stderr status line per job in text mode and no job progress; `--json` emits `job.progress` throttled by the emitter.
- Android telephony parsers (sub-project 4, plan 4D: `android.mmssms`, `android.calllog`, `android.contacts`):
  1. The DDL snapshots (`tools/fixtures/schemas/android-telephony/*.sql`) and the constants the parsers use (message and box types, call types, presentation, features, IM protocols, data types, MMS address types) were cross-checked against the public provider sources at the tag each snapshot cites, table by table (`CROSSCHECK.md`); a snapshot marked `historical` describes an older release or an OEM variant whose columns the sources define (the extra OEM columns of `mmssms-oem-extended.sql` are invented on purpose and named). The sources describe the platform, not a vendor build: a release or vendor that differs degrades per column (absent fields, `unmapped_columns`) or is `unsupported-schema`. No real device database was available (the `realdata` harness of plan 4H exists and no run is recorded, see "Real-data runs").
  2. The application's own deleted markers are read in the live parse (`raw_contacts.deleted`, the `deleted_contacts` table). All three parsers claim the tables their mappings map (plan 4G Tasks 9 and 10): `android.mmssms` claims `sms` and `pdu`, `android.calllog` claims `calls`, `android.contacts` claims `raw_contacts` and `deleted_contacts`. The rows of those tables that the recovery of the database yields from the freelist, old WAL frames or the journal are typed by the same mappings as `message`, `call` and `contact` records with `Deleted`, the row's recovery method and confidence cap and a recovered-row locator (`parse recover`, `sqlite recover`; the typed pass itself is Tasks 8 and 11 to 13). Only rows `ClaimMatch` accepts are typed (basis `schema`, or a strict `fit` that is unique among the live tables), so a typed record from a `fit` row (every freelist row) may come from a dropped table of the same shape as `sms` or `pdu` (`TestFitDecoyTypedRecordIsMarkedFitOnly`: the record says `table_basis: fit` and `identity-by-fit-only`, confidence at most 60), and a column reordering of the same affinities after a drop and recreate cannot be detected by a fit. A recovered `pdu` joins its `addr` and `part` rows in the LIVE view, so a message whose address rows were deleted with it comes back with `participants_unknown`; a recovered raw contact joins its `data`, `accounts` and `contacts` rows in the LIVE view, so one whose data rows were deleted with it comes back with its display name and source id but no phones or e-mails, and one with no name, no source id and no data is not a record but a counted unmapped warning (`TestNamelessRecoveredContactIsUnmappedNotRejected`: it identifies nobody). A recovered call or tombstone carries no join, and a recovered call that lost its rowid maps unchanged because the call mapping never reads the rowid (`TestRecoveredCallWithLostRowidMapsWithoutInventingAnId`); a recovered contact that lost it is unmapped (`TestRecoveredContactWithLostRowidIsUnmappedNotDropped`). Only whole freed pages are typed (4G-R22): a row deleted inside a page that stays in the tree is slack and stays `db_row`. A freed `deleted_contacts` tombstone is a `[rowid, integer]` record that fits several live tables, so the library cannot name its table and it stays a generic `db_row` (`TestFreedTombstonesAreNotTypedForWantOfATable`); a tombstone version superseded or deleted in a WAL is typed (basis `schema`).
  3. `Probe` reads the main file only; with a WAL or journal present its schema findings are deferred to `Parse`; the probe read limit applies. A table the probe would refuse (for example an `accounts` table whose `_id` is not the rowid alias) therefore reaches `Parse` when a companion exists, where the join is undecided and stated (`raw.join_duplicate` for a repeated key).
  4. Time rules: `sms.date` and `date_sent` below 1e11 ms are invalid (spec 9.1); call timestamps use plain `ts` with NO such guard (ruling T7: a seconds value in a millisecond column reads as a 1970 date); `pdu` times are seconds; a wrong device clock is not corrected. A raw contact has no time of its own in the provider: the only time the contacts parser states is the aggregate contact's `contact_last_updated_timestamp` (through `raw_contacts.contact_id`), under `times.aggregate_last_updated`, never as `Record.Time` (rulings T69, T73); it is read as milliseconds and a seconds value reads as a 1970 date.
  5. Contact accounts come only from the `accounts` join (`raw_contacts.account_id`); the inline `account_name`/`account_type` columns of older schemas are honoured only when physically present. A negative INTEGER `subscription_id` in the call log stays in `raw` unflagged (ruling Task 11, minor 5).
  6. Attachments are linked by the exact logical path `android:<part._data>`: a path with `*`, backslash, NUL, empty, `.` or `..` elements or more than 64 elements cannot be written as a glob and stays unlinked; a path matching only artifacts of another device, partition or snapshot stays unlinked (`raw.attachments_other_source`; the parse contract does not expose the acquisition id, so a second acquisition of the same device is not told apart); a path matching several artifacts of the same source stays unlinked (`raw.attachments_ambiguous`); an unlinked attachment means "not found in the case", not "no file"; part bytes are never read or hashed by the parser; the sender placeholder `insert-address-token` is kept as stored; a group MMS body joins its text parts with one `\n`.
  7. Caps (D9): 256 KiB body, 1000 list entries, 100 contact notes (`raw.notes_total` and `raw.notes_truncated` say so); a contact, message or thread whose joined rows exceed the budget fails the job with `parse.ErrBudget` (a typed refusal); the runner rotates the mapping Context at half of the budget (at most once per 1000 rows), and the join notes of rotated Contexts share note keys (the sink keeps the first), so join counts in `parse.job.end` are a lower bound. The contacts parser does one `accounts` and one `contacts` lookup per raw contact (not cached per parse; backlog T78).
  8. Altered free text is listed by field in `raw.text_flags`; the original bytes are in the artifact only (no per-field `raw_b64` except bodies, addresses and numbers).
  9. The runner ends a database of another kind as a clean, audited "not applicable at parse time" (one `analysis.warning`, note `not_applicable`, no records) because the host has no distinct job outcome for it yet (D13).
  10. The fixtures are generated on the engine version recorded in each `expect.json`; regenerating on another Python/SQLite build changes the database bytes (the pins change with them); `tools/fixtures/telephony_gen.py` needs Python 3 only, no Docker. The `duplicate-keys` contacts variant replaces the pinned `accounts` and `contacts` definitions with plain `_id integer` tables on purpose (T79).
- Browser history parsers (sub-project 4, plan 4F: `chromium.history`, `safari.history`):
  1. Chromium's DDL and constants are cross-checked against the public sources at a pinned release tag (`tools/fixtures/schemas/chromium/CROSSCHECK.md`); the older variants are historical, and two are not real files: `chromium-mid` (`meta.version` 48 to 49) combines the migration's `visits` shape with the tag's current downloads text and matches no real file, and `chromium-old` is synthetic (it omits the downloads tables), so neither is evidence of what a real older database holds. Safari is from memory (item 2).
  2. Safari's `History.db` tables, columns and the meanings of `origin`, `generation`, `attributes`, `score`, `synthesized` and `http_non_get` are from memory (no public schema exists to check them against); the fixtures are built from the same memory, so they prove only that the parser reads the remembered layout. A real database that differs is `unsupported-schema` or `not-applicable`, or has absent fields. The package-to-browser table of the Chromium parser (`com.android.chrome` is `chrome`, and so on) is from memory too, and WebView `History` files are matched by path although their existence on real devices is unverified. No real device database was available (spec layer 4: the `realdata` harness of plan 4H exists and no run is recorded, see "Real-data runs"); running the parsers over real databases is required before relying on them.
  3. The live parse reads live data and the application's own tombstones only (Safari `history_tombstones`). `Meta.Claims` equals `ClaimableTables()` (plan 4G Task 10b, `TestClaimsEqualClaimableTables`): `chromium.history` claims `visits`, `keyword_search_terms` and `downloads`, `safari.history` claims `history_visits`; the other mapped tables emit only on a decided condition and are not claimed. The rows of those tables that the recovery of the database yields from the freelist, old WAL frames or the journal are typed by the same mappings as `web_visit`, `web_search` and `download` records with `Deleted`, the row's recovery method and confidence cap and a recovered-row locator (`parse recover`, `sqlite recover`). Only rows `ClaimMatch` accepts are typed, so a typed record from a `fit` row (every freelist row) may come from a dropped table of the same shape (`identity-by-fit-only`, at most 60), and a visit joins its URL row, a search its URL row and a download its chain rows in the LIVE view (`joins-from-live-view`). A recovered visit whose live URL row is gone (cleared history) is a typed row error: an unmapped warning in `parse recover` and a `db_row` beside its warning in `sqlite recover` (4G-R21; `chromium-deleted` and `deleted` list them under `unmapped`); a recovered visit that lost its rowid has no `visit_id` and says `raw.unknown_columns.id = rowid-lost` (Safari 1.0.2 states it like Chromium). A non-finite REAL in a recovered Safari `visit_time` is stated by `visit_time_ieee` and `visit_time_status` invalid (P-4-1, `TestRecoveredNonFiniteVisitTimeIsStatedByItsBits`); a REAL in a Chromium integer column is `type-mismatch` in `unknown_columns` with its IEEE bits in `raw.<key>_ieee` (16 lowercase hex digits; a joined `urls` cell uses its `urls.<col>` key; `TestRealInAnIntegerColumnKeepsItsBits`, `TestJoinedRealInAnIntegerColumnKeepsItsBits`), and a stored NaN reads as NULL as the engine reads it, so it states nothing (`TestStoredNaNInAnIntegerColumnReadsAsNull`); the bits have an independent oracle in the `minimal` variant (REAL `visit_count` 1.25 on a joined URL and 2.5 on a visitless one), and the other seven parsers still state such a cell as `type-mismatch` without bits (backlog: a shared `rowrun` helper). The key is the one of the cell's own record: a visit prefixes its joined `urls` cells (`urls.<col>`), but a search record has no such scheme, so a REAL in the joined `urls.last_visit_time` of a search is flagged `unknown_columns.last_visit_time` with `raw.last_visit_time_ieee` and gives the search no time (`TestSearchWithARealURLLastVisitTimeStatesItsBitsUnprefixed`). In-page deletions are `db_row` only.
  4. A visit without a usable URL (the URL row is missing, empty or not text) is a counted warning, not a record (4F-R4), and on recovered data such rows are common. A Chromium download with neither a usable target path nor a chain URL is a counted warning.
  5. Visitless rules: a URL row or Safari item with no visit is emitted as visitless only on a decided miss. The required joins are by rowid (`MapContext.Get`); the information joins use the decoder's index, capped at 1,000,000 entries per joined column, and over the cap the information is undecided (stated as `join_undecided`, never as visitless). The visitless check costs one indexed scan of the visits table, charged to the budget; over its cap every such row is undecided.
  6. The browser label comes from the package directory of the logical path (the last `app_chrome` or `app_webview` segment decides); Chrome on iOS and other locations need `--artifact`. A search's time is its URL's last visit (`time_source` says so) and engine names are not resolved (they live in another database).
  7. Zero means "not recorded": a Chromium `total_bytes` of 0 gives no `total_bytes` and `raw.total_bytes = 0`, so a genuinely empty download cannot be told from "size not recorded", and both are stated that way. An undecodable `meta` value is `unknown`. Caps: URLs, titles and bodies are cut and flagged; a download chain is capped at 1000 entries and 2 MiB of URL text and the body at 1 MiB, with the totals stated.
  8. The duplicate chain index of a download (`duplicate-index`) cannot be shown by a conforming fixture, because `downloads_url_chains` has `PRIMARY KEY (id, chain_index)`; it is exercised by a Go test that builds the chain in memory (a hostile file may still carry one: `Probe` does not require the key).
  9. The fixtures depend on the engine that wrote them: regenerating on another Python/SQLite build changes the database bytes, and the WAL pins never regenerate byte-for-byte. `FuzzParseChromium` and `FuzzParseSafari` are harness-bound (measured 0.1 and 0.45 execs/s: a case is built per exec); the row-level fuzzers and the deterministic page-level and WAL header mutation tests carry the hostile-input evidence.

- plist decoding losses and refusals: `Decode` returns plain values; `Unarchive` knows the listed classes only and turns any other class into a map. A duplicate key in a dict keeps the last value, as the format libraries do, and is counted: `DecodeReport` returns it in `Report.DuplicateKeys` (per occurrence in the expanded tree, and not inside an entry that a later entry has already replaced; binary and XML documents, and `NSDictionary` in `Unarchive`, where it is counted once per resolved object (`UnarchiveReport`; the strict `Unarchive` refuses such an archive with the same `*LossError`, after the whole archive resolved); for XML every `<key>` of the document is counted, also inside an entry a later entry replaced, so the XML count can exceed the binary one for the same tree, and it is still a lower bound) and the strict `Decode` refuses such a document with a `*LossError` wrapping `ErrUnsupported`; the XML duplicate check holds one set of key texts per open dictionary and is charged up front (`chargePerXMLNode` per node, which also covers the library's own per-element allocation; see the memory charge below). Two consecutive XML `<key>` elements are `ErrMalformed`, an archived `NSSet`/`NSMutableSet` is a `Set` and an ordered set a `Set{Ordered: true}` (items in archive order, uniqueness not checked), while a binary set tag and a binary null are `ErrMalformed` (the decoding library refuses them; they are never read as something else). `Unarchive` keeps `NS.time` as stored (float64 for a stored real, int64 or uint64 for a stored integer), and the raw seconds are never turned into a time (that is `decode/ts`). A binary date within one float step of the 0001 or 10000 edge is judged on the stored double but may come back from the library rounded, so its `Seconds` can differ by one step from the stored value there; an XML out-of-range date's `Seconds` is computed from the text; a dict key that is not valid text is `ErrUnsupported` (a non-string `NSDictionary` key too). The XML pre-scan is conservative and may refuse exotic but valid plists (UTF-16 XML, a DOCTYPE with an internal subset); OpenStep and GNUstep text plists are refused. `uint64` appears only from XML or a 16-byte positive integer (an 8-byte value with the top bit set is an int64 by the format rule), integers or UIDs beyond 64 bits are `ErrUnsupported` and a negative `CF$UID` is `ErrMalformed`. The memory charge (`len(b)*8 + nodes*478 + payload*2`, plus `nodes*1621` for an XML document, so an XML node is charged 2099 B; each total per node carries a 25 percent margin over the worst measured cost under `-race`, 382.4 B binary and 1678.6 B XML) is a model held to a measurement: `TestChargeCoversMeasuredAllocation` requires it to be at least the bytes allocated while decoding 28 shapes in binary and XML (52 shape and format pairs: flat, many small maps, one large map, empty and small dictionaries, dictionaries of 9 and 17 keys, empty arrays, nesting of 30 and 60 levels, booleans, dates, reals, wide integers, UIDs, short and long strings, UTF-16, non-ASCII and emoji strings, long keys, data, XML attribute strings, comments and whitespace-only text), also under `-race`, with the measured worst case at 0.79 of the charge (0.82 under `-race`). Shapes the decoder refuses (a binary set, an XML processing instruction) have no measurement because nothing is allocated for them beyond the validation. `Unarchive` is charged per resolved object, `1195 + payload bytes + 36 per map entry and list slot` of its fields (`TestUnarchiveChargeCoversMeasuredAllocation`, 12 archive shapes, none allocating more than 0.80 of its charge, also under `-race`), so under the default 1 GiB budget an archive of more than about 898,000 objects (about 780,000 with 5 map entries or slots each) is refused with `ErrNoBudget`. The objects are charged one by one: an over-cap archive is refused only after objects up to the budget have been built and dropped, so peak memory reaches the budget but never exceeds it; the charge is freed and nothing is returned. A shape it was not measured on and the process's peak outside a decode are not covered. Because the charge is that large, the default 1 GiB memory budget refuses the biggest documents the caps admit (`MaxInput` 64 MiB, `MaxNodes` 1 Mi): an XML document must satisfy `8*len + 2099*nodes + 2*payload <= 1 GiB`, so it has at most 511,549 nodes even if it were empty (about 475,000 at 20 bytes per node); a binary document must satisfy `8*len + 478*nodes + 2*payload <= 1 GiB`, so a 1 Mi-node binary document fits only while `8*len + 2*payload` stays within 546 MiB (a 64 MiB document then leaves room for 17 MiB of string and data payload). A larger document is refused with `ErrNoBudget` before anything is allocated (`TestMaxDocumentChargeIsRefusedNotCut`), never decoded in part or cut; a host that must decode them raises the memory budget. No production caller decodes plists today (`internal/ios/mb2` uses only `PrescanXML` and `Check`).

- Calendar parsers (plan 4I3; the `calendar_event` payload of `internal/recordtypes/calendar`):
  1. An event dated outside 1970 to 2099 has no `Time` and no date (Q5, coordinator ruling 5): the timestamp decoder guards that range only, so a stored start or end before 1970 (an all-day 1955 birthday, a historical anniversary) or after 2099 gives no `Time`, `TimeEnd`, `start_date` or `end_date`; the stored value is kept in raw with its `invalid` status, never shifted into range and never presented as a date.
  2. `android.calendar` reads only the SQLite `calendar.db` of user versions 400 to 699 (B4): 403, 502, 600 and 601 have cross-checked fixtures, and an ICS export, an older schema or any user version outside the band has no fixture and is refused (`unsupported-schema` at the provider path, `not-applicable` elsewhere, `ErrUnsupportedVersion` from Parse when a companion deferred the check), never read as if it were a known schema. A version inside the band that no fixture covers is read with the columns it has (every `Events` column but the five identifying ones is optional).
  3. All-day values not at a UTC midnight have no date (spec 5.2): the provider stores an all-day event at a UTC midnight, so a value off midnight gives no `Time`, no `start_date` (or no `TimeEnd` and no `end_date`) and `raw.start_state` (or `end_state`) `all-day-not-midnight`, and is never repaired by a zone; an all-day row whose `eventTimezone` is not `UTC` is still read from its UTC midnight and states `raw.all_day_zone_state` `not-utc`. Zone names are never applied: a timed event is a utc instant with its stored zone name beside it, an all-day date is a `local-unknown` reading, and a consumer that needs local wall-clock times must apply the zone itself. A computed end (`dtstart + duration`) past the timestamp range has no `TimeEnd` and states `raw.end_state` `out-of-range`.
  4. Recurring series are one record at their first occurrence (spec 6.1): the rule (`rrule`, `rdate`, `exrule`, `exdate`) is kept as stored and never expanded, so the later occurrences of a series and the provider's `Instances` cache of expanded occurrences are not records; an exception row is its own record linked by `exception_of`.

  7. `android.calendar` keeps `CalendarAlerts`, `ExtendedProperties` and `EventsRawTimes` in raw only (spec 7.3, Q8): an alert that fired (`state` 1, `state_label` `fired`) is evidence that a reminder was shown, but it is not a record of its own and is not on the timeline; an extended property's value is cut at 4 KiB (`value_flags`), its name is an identifier (over 1 KiB not kept, its length and SHA-256 stated); each list keeps 1,000 entries per event with its total, and only those first 1,000 rows are read, so an unreadable or wrongly typed cell of a later alert or property is not listed in `unknown_columns` (the total still counts its row). The `CalendarMetaData` and `CalendarCache` values (`localTimezone`, `minInstance`, `maxInstance`, `timezoneType`, `timezoneInstances`, `timezoneInstancesPrevious`) are notes as stored and are never applied to any time; the `Instances` cache is counted (`rows.Instances`) and named in `unmapped_tables`, never read.

Typed recovery (spec 10, B2): `android.calendar` claims `Events` only, so a recovered attendee, reminder, alert, extended property or calendar row alone is no record (the generic `db_row` of the recovery run keeps it). A recovered event joins no table, even when rows that name it survive in the live view: because a reused row id would attach another event's children, the record carries no calendar, attendees or reminders and states each join `not-joined-recovered`, so a recovered event has less context than a live one and its calendar's deletion state is not evaluated.

Such a value is not at a UTC midnight, so under item 11 the record has no `TimeEnd` and no `end_date`, and the stored value stays in `raw` with `end_state` `all-day-not-midnight`. One-day data cannot tell an inclusive last day from the start plus the days less a second on a multi-day event, so no end date is derived (that would be interpretation); a consumer that needs the end reads the stored value in raw. How this store writes a multi-day all-day event is not known.
  15. Attendees are unmapped on a real iOS 13 `Participant` layout (B12, run 3 of "Real-data runs"): that table has `owner_id` and `entity_type` and no `item_id`, and it had no rows, so nothing shows what `owner_id` names; the `entity_type` column suggests more than one kind of owner. `ios.calendar` therefore does not join attendees by `owner_id`: each event states `raw.join_undecided` for `Participant` and the run counts it in one `calendar-join-undecided` warning, never "no attendees", so on such a store no event has attendees. A store whose `Participant` has `item_id` (the remembered layout, and the fixtures) is read as before, and how it relates to the real layout is unknown.
  16. Cross-parser backlog (Q10, B2): rows recovered from free pages, journals and WAL history join to no other table in either calendar parser. A recovered record states each join `not-joined-recovered` (items 8 and 13), because a row id that was freed and reused would attach another row's children, and no shared layer says whether a table reuses row ids (no AUTOINCREMENT detection exists in `sqlitefile`, `decode/sqlitedb` or the parse interface). Until one does, a join of a recovered row is a guess and is not made; the recovered record has less context than a live one. Closing this needs a shared, tested way to tell that a row id cannot have been reused, and it stands as a backlog item that no plan owns.
  17. Size of one event (final review I2): both calendar parsers bound every part of a record on its own: free texts are cut (4 KiB, the description 256 KiB), identifiers over their limit and a value kept in raw only as stored over 16 KiB are not kept but stated by their length and SHA-256 (`<key>_state` `too-long`), and each child list keeps 1,000 entries. Those bounds do not bound their sum: an event whose bounded parts together exceed the records cap of 16 MiB on the payload (for example 1,000 iOS recurrence rows of five 16 KiB stored values each) is refused by the host as too large, rejected and counted (`rejected`, audited as an `analysis.warning`), so that event is not in the case.

The blob's own `date` is raw only. Secret chats are recorded and flagged, never decrypted or keyed (spec 9, call 6, T1): a secret-chat message is a record with `raw.secret_chat`, `raw.enc_chat_id` and the Summary channel `telegram secret`, its counterparty from `enc_chats.user` only; `enc_chats.data` (TL EncryptedChat) is not decoded and no other `enc_chats` column is interpreted, so the chat's state, layer, TTL and key exchange are not stated.
